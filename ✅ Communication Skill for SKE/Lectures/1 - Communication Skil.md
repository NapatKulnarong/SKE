## 1. Definitions

**Communication** is the *process* of exchanging information, ideas, thoughts, or feelings. Channels:

| Type | Examples |
| --- | --- |
| **Verbal** | Meetings, standups, calls |
| **Non-verbal** | Eye contact, posture, tone |
| **Written** | Emails, docs, tickets |
| **Visual** | Diagrams, graphs |
| **Digital** | Chat, issue trackers |

**Skill** is the *learned ability* to do something well (practice, not a definition).

- **Hard skills** — technical (coding, accounting)
- **Soft skills** — used with people (leadership, adaptability, **communication**)

**Communication skill** is sending *and* receiving clearly: listen, speak, write, match tone/body language, and adapt to the audience.

> Communication = process. Skill = learned ability. Communication skill = doing the process well.

Technical work that nobody can explain, review, or follow still fails.

## 2. LSRW

**LSRW** = Listening, Speaking, Reading, Writing. Two axes:

| | Receptive (take in) | Productive (put out) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Auditory** | Listening | Speaking |
| **Textual** | Reading | Writing |

This course trains the auditory pair first. Both pairs are required.

## 3. Why English

Speaker counts (millions; Wikipedia ranking used in class):

| # | Language | L1 | L2 | Total |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | English | 390 | **1,138** | **1,528** |
| 2 | Mandarin | **990** | 194 | 1,184 |
| 29 | Thai | 27 | 44 | 71 |

- Mandarin wins on **native** speakers. English wins **overall** because of **L2** — it is the lingua franca for people who do not share a first language.
- L1 = first language; L2 = learned later. Engineering English is an L2 phenomenon.
- MSA Arabic is listed with 0 L1 (a learned standard). Same pattern as English/Indonesian/Swahili: more L2 than L1.
- Thai is local (~71M). Papers, libraries, conferences, and pitches in this field default to English.

A **conference** is the destination: listen (receptive) and present (productive) in that shared language.

## 4. How to present

For SKE, speaking/listening moves **technical meaning** (design, requirements, demos) — not “English class.” Q&A is part of the talk.

A good presentation **tells a clear story** and **sticks**. Seven rules:

1. **Purpose** — inform, persuade, teach, or propose. One goal, one sentence.
2. **Audience** — technical vs not. Change tone, words, examples.
3. **Structure**
   - *Intro:* hook, purpose, outline
   - *Body:* 2–4 points, examples/data, no overload
   - *Close:* summary + strong message or CTA
4. **Slides** — visuals over paragraphs; ~5–6 bullets, ~6 words each; consistent layout; **don’t read them**
5. **Delivery** — clear speech, eye contact (or camera), natural gestures, vary pitch
6. **Time** — stay in the limit; timer; don’t rush the ending
7. **Engage** — questions, short interaction if it fits, **leave Q&A time**

## 5. Self-introduction

Short, spoken, with a hook — not a form recitation.

Reference: [Tell me about yourself (mmmEnglish)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Tj1w86bw4EM)

Include: name (“I’m …”), place, study/role, one fact that invites a follow-up.

| Format           | Job                   | Length / tone         |
| ---------------- | --------------------- | --------------------- |
| **Resume**       | One role              | ~1 page, selective    |
| **CV**           | Full record           | Longer, comprehensive |
| **Presentation** | Live first impression | ~1–2 min, one story   |

Do not read a resume off slides. Same structure as §4: open → 2–3 points → close.

## Takeaways

- Communication skill = practiced two-way process, not a personality trait.
- LSRW = auditory/textual × receptive/productive.
- English is the working language because of **L2**, not native population.
- Talks succeed on purpose, audience, structure, slides, delivery, time, and engagement.
