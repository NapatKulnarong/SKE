# Mock — Risk Assessment Workshop

**Source:** [[5 - Risk Assessment Workshop]]  
**Format:** 45 multiple choice. One best answer. Answers at the end.

---

**1.** This lecture is a **workshop**, not a definition list. Most risks found are:

- A. Hacking / pentest risks
- B. **Privacy and legal risks**, not hacking risks
- C. AES key-size risks
- D. Feistel round counts

**2.** The educational “project” is set on:

- A. Thailand only
- B. **Planet Astra** (not a real person or organization)
- C. The EU exclusively
- D. Microsoft SDL

**3.** The province has **20,000** secondary students across **77 schools**. The committee wants **PHQ-A** data. PHQ-A is:

- A. A 12-requirement PCI standard
- B. The adolescent **9-item** depression screener (`Q1`–`Q9`, scored 0–3); last item is **self-harm / better off dead**
- C. A Caesar cipher
- D. ASVS Level 1

**4.** This is described as **health data about minors** —:

- A. Low sensitivity
- B. The **most sensitive combination** you can be handed
- C. Only PII, not health data
- D. Anonymized by default

**5.** The **survey** goal (“5,000 of 20,000 at risk”) needs **aggregate statistics** and has **low** privacy cost. The **intervention** goal (“help children who scored badly”) needs:

- A. No identifiers
- B. To **identify specific students** — **high** privacy cost
- C. Only ROT13
- D. Only a DAST scan

**6.** Conflicting privacy requirements cannot be resolved by **“do both.”** They must be **separated, scoped, or escalated** back to the committee. This conflict appears in the **requirements** phase, which is:

- A. Too late; wait until the database is built
- B. **Exactly where SSDLC says risk assessment belongs**
- C. Only a testing concern
- D. Solved by RC4

**7.** Discovering the conflict **after the database is built** is a **redesign**. Discovering it **now** is:

- A. A pentest
- B. A **conversation**
- C. k-anonymity
- D. A Root CA

**8.** In the naive schema, everything before the `Q` columns is **PII**, sitting in the **same table** as answers. **Direct identification** means:

- A. You need a quantum computer
- B. **Name + school + grade** identifies a child with **no effort**
- C. Only UUID is identifying
- D. Only zip code is identifying

**9.** **Sensitive-by-association** means joined to `Q1`–`Q9` the row is:

- A. Still just a student record
- B. A **diagnosable mental health profile of a named minor**
- C. Aggregate-only
- D. A trade secret

**10.** **One breach = total loss** because:

- A. There is a partial failure mode
- B. There is **no partial failure mode**; leaking the table leaks **identity and diagnosis together**
- C. Only names leak
- D. Only Q9 leaks

**11.** Health data is **sensitive personal data** under **PDPA Art. 26 / GDPR Art. 9**, which means:

- A. Weaker consent, smaller penalties
- B. **Stricter consent and larger penalties**
- C. No law applies on Planet Astra
- D. Only APPI Art. 26

**12.** The **survey** goal needs **none** of the PII columns. Collecting them anyway violates **data minimization** **before** any attacker. The design lesson:

- A. Schema is only a performance choice
- B. **The schema is a policy document**; what you put in one table decides what a single mistake can cost
- C. UUID columns are never PII-adjacent
- D. Teachers cannot coerce

**13.** Students are data subjects but **furthest from every decision** and the only ones with **no institutional power**. Infrastructure belongs to a **third party** (another university’s **Google Workspace**), so whoever owns the account owns:

- A. Only PHQ-A scoring
- B. **Access logs, admin console, and effectively the data**
- C. Only parental consent
- D. Only AES keys

**14.** **Teachers** physically collect answers and see students every day. That is:

- A. Only a technical SAST issue
- B. A **coercion channel** and a **confidentiality leak** at the same time
- C. Proof they are the controller
- D. k-anonymity

**15.** Nobody in the stakeholder map has been **formally designated as legally responsible**. That gap becomes:

- A. §2 naive schema
- B. **§8 organizational roles**
- C. §7 Hunt the Witch only
- D. Classical ciphers

**16.** Before writing a consent form you must know **age** (who can consent), **jurisdiction** (PDPA/GDPR/APPI), **what data**, **purpose**, and **retention** as a **stated number**, not:

- A. Purpose-bound
- B. **“Indefinitely”**
- C. Minimal
- D. Sufficient for the goal

**17.** Collect **nothing “just in case.”** Consent is **purpose-bound**; a new purpose needs:

- A. A UUID
- B. **New consent**
- C. Only a DPO email
- D. A Feistel round

**18.** You **cannot write the consent form until the design is settled**, because it must **describe the design accurately**. An unclear design produces:

- A. Stronger AES
- B. An **unlawful** consent form
- C. Better k-anonymity
- D. A processor agreement automatically

**19.** **Legally**, below the statutory age the child cannot consent alone — **parents** consent. **PDPA: 10**, **GDPR: 16** (lowerable to 13). **Ethically**, the child must also:

- A. Be the controller
- B. **Assent**
- C. Host Google Workspace
- D. Sign the DPA

**20.** The committee’s “parental consent only — no child consent needed” is **legally coherent**, but:

- A. Also always ethically sufficient
- B. **Legal sufficiency is not ethical sufficiency**
- C. Illegal under all laws
- D. The same as anonymization

**21.** Parental consent authorizes the **research**; it does **not** make the child a **willing participant**. On a private mental-health instrument, the child’s cooperation **is** the **data source**, so willingness is:

- A. Optional
- B. **Not optional**
- C. Replaced by gift cards
- D. A quasi-identifier

**22.** Extra credit and **gift cards** are **inducements**: they make refusal costly, so consent is no longer:

- A. Purpose-bound
- B. **Freely given**
- C. Jurisdictional
- D. A linkage attack

**23.** “The parents consented, so it’s fine” turns legal permission into:

- A. k-anonymity
- B. An **ethical blind spot**
- C. A data processing agreement
- D. Anonymization

**24.** If you **force** children to participate: it is a **child-rights violation** regardless of paperwork, and **data validity collapses** because students will mark **randomly**, answer **defensively**, or hide symptoms — especially on:

- A. UUID
- B. **`Q9`**
- C. Zip code
- D. Grade band `m5`

**25.** Ethics and data quality here **point the same way**. Coercion does not buy better coverage; it buys:

- A. Perfect random samples
- B. **Noise you cannot detect**
- C. GDPR Art. 9 exemptions automatically
- D. A Root CA

**26.** In the kidnap/escape-room thought experiment, **consent causes self-selection bias** is:

- A. False
- B. A **true premise** and a real methodological problem
- C. The smuggled step
- D. The absurd conclusion

**27.** The **actual error** is the **smuggled step**: a **methodological** need can outweigh a **rights** constraint. The absurd conclusion (kidnapping) makes that step visible. Better data is something you **want**; consent is:

- A. Optional for high L×E
- B. A **line you don’t cross**
- C. Only for adults
- D. The same as DAST

**28.** The same move without the cartoon: “don’t tell users we’re logging” / “make it opt-out and bury it.” Legitimate bias remedies include report response rates, weight/stratify, ethics-board **waiver-of-consent for low-risk** studies, or **aggregate-only** data. **Removing consent**:

- A. Is one of those remedies
- B. **Is not** one of those remedies
- C. Fixes response bias
- D. Assigns the processor

**29.** Coercion **doesn’t fix** selection bias; it swaps it for **response bias**, which is:

- A. Easier to detect
- B. **Harder to detect**
- C. Required by PHQ-A
- D. A CA chain

**30.** A typical AI answer can be **right** because the example is **extreme**, yet it still writes a fluent **“For”** section. Rephrase (“devil’s advocate”) and **For** gets longer. Lesson: be suspicious of **“good research requires we skip a safeguard.”** An AI:

- A. Supplies your ethical floor
- B. Will argue **either side** fluently; refusals track how **obviously bad** the example looks — **you** bring the ethical floor
- C. Cannot argue the “For” side
- D. Replaces the DPO

**31.** **Pseudonymization** replaces identifiers with a code **while the mapping still exists**. It is **still reversible** — and **must be**, if the goal is to **help individual children**. Under GDPR, pseudonymized data is:

- A. Outside the law
- B. **Still personal data** and fully regulated
- C. Defined the same way in APPI (Lecture 2: APPI does **not** define it)
- D. The same as k-anonymity

**32.** **True irreversible anonymization** leaves the scope of the law but is **incompatible with**:

- A. Aggregate statistics
- B. **Individual follow-up**
- C. Quasi-identifiers
- D. Teachers collecting forms

**33.** **Hunt the Witch**: targeted re-identification differs from bulk profiling. Knowing **grade, gender, and which school** the batch came from, filtering often leaves:

- A. 20,000 rows
- B. **One row**
- C. No rows
- D. Only Q9

**34.** **Aggregation doesn’t save you when the group is small.** “3 of 5 students in this school are at risk”:

- A. Is fully anonymous
- B. Is a statistic that **identifies people**; **small cells leak**
- C. Requires system access
- D. Is a Feistel property

**35.** Linkage example: join voter list (Name, Gender, Zip) to “anonymized” insurance (Gender, Zip, Disease). Only one male in zip `10800` → **H has testicular cancer**. **Quasi-identifiers** identify nobody alone but almost everybody **in combination** (gender, zip, birth date, school, grade). The attacker needs:

- A. Admin on your Google Workspace
- B. **No access to your system** — they attack the **published/shared** version
- C. The private RSA key
- D. Q9 only

**36.** Defenses listed: suppress small cells, **coarsen** (grade band not grade, region not zip), **minimum group size (k-anonymity)**, or **don’t release row-level data**. Publishing **per-school PHQ-A** breakdowns is a linkage attack waiting for:

- A. AES-256
- B. A **school directory — which is public**
- C. A DPO
- D. ROT13

**37.** **Marketing or targeting people in the EU** brings **GDPR** regardless of where you are (GDPR follows the **data subject**; PDPA follows **presence in Thailand**). Data **crossing borders** is its own regulated act. Here, Workspace belongs to **another university**, so data likely sits:

- A. Only on student laptops
- B. On **servers in another country**, under another regime, accessible to an admin who **never signed your consent form**
- C. Only in RAM
- D. Only in a Root CA

**38.** Consequence of jurisdiction: **two or more legal regimes may apply at once**, and:

- A. The weakest one governs
- B. The **strictest** one governs your design
- C. None apply on Planet Astra
- D. Only APPI applies

**39.** **Data Controller** decides **why** and **how**. In this project that is the **Research Committee**. **Processor** processes **on behalf of** the controller (e.g. cloud). In this project the processor is:

- A. The student
- B. **Unassigned**
- C. Always the teacher
- D. PHQ-A itself

**40.** **Teachers** are described as **Data Collector** (functional, not always a formal legal role). **DPO** oversees compliance. **Why ambiguity is a risk:** if nobody is controller, nobody answers **access/rectification/erasure** (statutory **deadlines**). Roles follow **what you actually do**, not your title. “I was just the advisor” is not a defence if you decided **purpose and schema**. Google as processor needs a **data processing agreement**, which:

- A. Was already written
- B. **Nobody has written**
- C. Replaces consent
- D. Is k-anonymity

**41.** Using **Risk = Likelihood × Exposure**, **PII + PHQ-A in one table** is L=M, **E=H**. Mitigation: **split tables**, **pseudonymize**, restrict the mapping. **Re-identification via quasi-identifiers** is:

- A. L=L, E=L
- B. **L=H, E=H**
- C. Unscored
- D. Only a hacking risk

**42.** **Invalid consent** (coerced child, parent-only form) is **L=H, E=M**. Mitigation: **parental consent + child assent** and a genuine right to decline. **Scope creep** from survey into individual tracking is **L=H, E=H** because:

- A. AES failed
- B. **Purpose-bound consent is silently violated**
- C. DES is broken
- D. Teachers are Root CAs

**43.** Mitigation for scope creep: separate the **two goals** into **two datasets** with **two consents**. Cross-border Workspace is L=M, E=M → DPA and **decide hosting deliberately**. Unassigned roles is **L=H, E=M** → **assign roles in writing before collection**.

- A. After the first leak
- B. **Before collection starts**
- C. Only if GDPR Art. 9 is ignored
- D. Only for AES

**44.** The **architectural** answer: **don’t build one system that does both**. **Aggregate** dataset = genuinely anonymized, safe to analyze/publish. **Intervention** pathway = separate, tightly controlled, own consent, access control, **retention limit**. Re-identification becomes:

- A. A default property of the schema
- B. An **act**: deliberate, authorized, and **logged**
- C. Impossible under PDPA
- D. The same as DAST

**45.** Organizational risks (unclear roles, unmanaged third-party infra, cross-border transfer) have **no technical fix** and are the ones that produce **fines**. Conflicting requirements are a **security finding**. A consent form must state data, purpose, retention, and legal basis for **age and jurisdiction**. **Pseudonymization reduces exposure** but does **not** remove you from the law or stop a **targeted** attacker.

- A. Only hacking is expensive
- B. **The unglamorous risks are the expensive ones**
- C. Gift cards fix Q9
- D. One table is always safer
